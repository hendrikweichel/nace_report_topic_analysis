## Executive Chairman's Report For personal use only

NORTHERN MINERALS ANNUAL REPORT 2022

## LETTER FROM THE EXECUTIVE CHAIRMAN

Fellow Shareholders,

## I have pleasure in presenting the 2022 Annual Report of Northern Minerals Limited.

The past year saw several, significant changes that transformed the Company, positioning it to take important steps towards establishing a commercial-scale beneficiation plant at Browns Range and to pursue development of the precinct to its fullest potential.

The Company's aspiration is to build its operation into a significant producer of ethically produced rare earth elements and separated products, including the heavy rare earths, dysprosium and terbium.

The neodynium iron-boron magnet (NdFeB) used in clean energy, military and high technology solutions contain the essential ingredients of dysprosium and terbium. Their unique characteristics enhance the performance of magnets at high temperatures and improve their resistance to demagnetisation. This is particularly desirable for magnets used in higher temperature operating conditions, such as large offshore wind turbines, electric vehicle motors, robotics in modern manufacturing, and energy-saving heating, ventilation, and air conditioning systems.

China is responsible for almost 100% of rare earth magnet manufacturing. While only holding some 35% of known rare earth reserves, it represents 55-60% of rare earth mining production, about 85% of midstream separation and metal/ refining capacity, and roughly 90% of NdFeB permanent magnet manufacturing.

Many equipment manufacturers in a wide variety of sectors are seeking to secure access to additional supply chains for strategic minerals, such as rare earths.

The Australian Government has created the Critical Minerals Facilitation Office while the US has created the Advanced Technology Vehicles Manufacturing Loan Program to provide support, including funding, for the development of critical mineral projects.

I believe Northern Minerals is well placed to take opportunities to participate in programs such as these. As one of only a few companies outside of China to have produced dysprosium and terbium, our Browns Range and other properties certainly qualify for consideration.

The strategic review, carried out by the Board during the year, confirmed that producing and marketing a mixed heavy rare earth concentrate from a commercial-scale beneficiation plant at Browns Range is the preferred near-term strategy, offering earlier access to cash flow and a lower risk profile.

The strategic review identified an opportunity for the Company to expedite production and bring forward cash flow from the high-grade Wolverine deposit at Browns Range. We have initiated steps to put together an experienced team to produce an updated Detailed Feasibility Study (DFS) for a commercial scale beneficiation plant, leveraging work undertaken in the earlier 2015 DFS. Included will be an assessment of the benefits (financial and otherwise) of alternative mining methods, inclusion of an ore sorter to increase product grade and de-risk mining dilution following the pilot plant achieving a 60% improvement in ore sorted product grade.

The Company began producing heavy rare earth carbonate in late 2018 as part of the pilot program. The program was aimed at assessing the economic and technical feasibility of a larger scale development at Browns Range. An ore sorter installed and commissioned during 2021 also tested for its economic and technical feasibility at the front end of the pilot plant. The Company completed the three-year test program on the Pilot Plant at Browns Range during the March 2022 quarter and the plant has been placed on care and maintenance.

Since inception the Company has produced 281 tonnes of carbonate from 1,140 tonnes of rare earth oxide concentrate from the Browns Range pilot plant.

## CHIEF EXECUTIVE OFFICER'S REVIEW

The Company completed the three-year test program on the Pilot Plant at Browns Range during the March 2022 quarter and the Pilot Plant has been placed on care and maintenance. This represents the successful culmination of the research and development project to assess the economic and technical feasibility of the heavy rare earth mine and process.

During the first half of calendar 2022, the Company undertook a strategic review of the Browns Range Project under the direction of the Chairman. The review identified significant financial and commercial benefits available to the Company from accelerating the development of the Wolverine orebody, which we believe contains the highest grades of Dysprosium and Terbium of any deposit in Australia.

Wolverine is contained within a specific shear zone that is part of the Browns Range system, which retains significant potential for further mineralised shear zones. Wolverine will underpin the proposed mining resource for the forthcoming bankable feasibility study.

The review also noted the strong and growing use of rare earth magnets, giving rise to growth in demand for the Company's key products. Together with our improved understanding of the Browns Range metallurgy following three years of processing through the pilot plant, these macro fundamentals provide us with the confidence to move towards producing a bankable feasibility study as a precursor to production of a rare earth concentrate rich in dysprosium and terbium. We are also investigating commercial arrangements for downstream processing.

Following the commissioning of the ore sorter system in FY2021, the Company focused on testing the system and producing ore-sorted material during the FY2022 year. This material was converted to a ~30% Total Rare Earth Oxide (TREO) xenotime concentrate through the pilot beneficiation plant then further processed through the hydrometallurgical plant to produce a rare earth carbonate (REC). A total of 106,106 kg of REC was shipped for sale to thyssenkrupp.

The results of the Pilot Plant test programs, including results from the ore sorter, will be utilised in the Detailed Feasibility Study (DFS) for a commercialscale beneficiation plant.

## Strategic imperative driving demand for rare earths

Understanding of the global significance of rare earths has continued to grow, and especially the need for secure alternative supply chains to mitigate the influence of China, which holds a dominant position in the manufacture of rare earth metal componentry for magnets. Heightened geopolitical tensions are driving both increased investment in modern armament and the implementation of policy initiatives to support the development of nonChinese sources of supply.

At the same time, product innovation and dramatic increases in community, industry and government demand for more sustainable energy solutions are contributing to the escalating demand for rare earths. Governments are increasingly requiring industry and companies to develop and deliver on plans to reduce carbon emissions, a process that has accelerated tremendously during the last year.

## Exploration

The Company's large landholdings in Western Australia and the Northern Territory are highly prospective for rare earths. The current portfolio consists of three projects, the flagship Browns Range Project and the John Galt Project in WA, and the Boulder Ridge Project located in the NT.

Browns Range currently has a JORC resource of over nine million tonnes averaging 0.67% TREO and 0.87% heavy rare earth oxides. Exploration to date has only examined approximately 15% of the Browns Range Dome, a large oval-shaped structure, approximately 60 kilometres by 30 kilometres, on the Western Australian Northern Territory border.

During the first half of FY2022, Northern Minerals conducted a drilling programme at Browns Range completing 18,650 (RC) Drilling, 3,100 metres of diamond drilling and 650 metres aircore drilling.

RC drilling targeted anomalous prospects within a 20km radius of the Browns Range pilot processing facility, whilst diamond drilling provided core samples from the Wolverine hanging wall for geotechnical and underground mine design purposes.

Encouraging drill results were received from the Cyclops, Rockslider and Banshee prospects.

## Environmental management

Northern Minerals is committed to producing ethical rare earths and integrating risk-based environmental management throughout its operations. We strive for continual improvement of our environmental performance, efficient use of resources, and prevention and mitigation of pollution. We consider high environmental standards a critical part of our operations.

Following the completion of the Browns Range Pilot Plant program in April 2022, Northern Minerals submitted the Care and Maintenance Plan to the Department of Mines Industry Regulation and Safety (DMIRS). Key commitments included closing abandonment bunds around Wolverine and Gambit West pits (completed in August 2022) and addressing ongoing surface water issues. The exploration team have been instrumental in getting this work done efficiently.

Significant progress was made in Q3 and Q4 2021 to improve roads and drainage around the site to direct surface water run-off back into the bush and reduce scouring of the roads. These works form part of the ongoing drainage improvement program.

Northern Minerals strives to meet and improve our ongoing environmental reporting obligations, both internal and external. During the year Northern Minerals:

- progressed the Environmental Management System (EMS) components of the Integrated Management System (IMS) manual in alignment with ISO14001;
- submitted all required annual environmental reports to relevant State Government regulators; and
- maintained internal reporting procedures by including environmental incidents and issues in daily site reports and including environmental performance in monthly reports to the Board.

Northern Minerals reported one incident in the annual environmental report to DMIRS in Q1 2022.  Several open sterilisation drill holes were discovered during a fauna survey in late 2021. Drilled in 2013/14, these holes had not been properly rehabilitated and subsequently re-opened. Following the discovery of the open drill holes, we undertook an audit of all 384 holes drilled for sterilisation and found 79 open holes. All open drill holes have now been stabilised and the collars rehabilitated.

In late 2021, hydrasleeves have been used for groundwater monitoring which resulted in considerable improvements. Hydrasleeves produce a more representative sample than bailers and pumps and reduce health and safety exposures by reducing the amount of manual work needed for the use of a generator and pump system. Further innovation in water sampling involved the use of a fishing drone to take water samples from 2 to 5 m in the centre of the open pit. This technique improves safety by removing the need to enter the open pit and provides better quality information through more representative sampling of the pit water quality. This information can better inform use and disposal of the waters when the pits are dewatered ahead of further mining.

In July 2022, we restarted environmental planning work and environmental studies to support long-lead time approvals. This work will be used to support the feasibility study for the full-scale beneficiation plant. Changes to the development envelope were finalised around the airstrip, tailings storage facility and site access road. A Miscellaneous Licence application has been submitted to DMIRS to widen the site access road corridor and to allow for additional linear infrastructure within the corridor.

Desktop flora and fauna studies have been commissioned and heritage survey planning has started. This work will support the application to amend the project development envelope  in the existing Ministerial Statement 986 under Part IV of the Environmental Protection Act.

NORTHERN MINERALS ANNUAL REPORT 2022

## COO REPORT

The past year represented a period of transition for Northern Minerals with the conclusion of the pilot plant program at Browns Range. The achievement of this milestone gave the company the opportunity to pause and review the strategy to becoming an internationally significant producer of heavy rare earths elements (HREE), in particular dysprosium and terbium.

Throughout 2022, and the preceding three years, Browns Range was the only significant producer of HREE outside China. Northern Minerals is extremely proud of this achievement and has spent 2022 focusing on refining the path to maintaining this status and potentially expanding our market footprint into the future.

Completion of the pilot plant program, along with all the hard work done over the last 12 years, has placed Northern Minerals in a unique position to build a business that positions itself as a key part of the supply chain for the growing rare earth permanent magnet industry.

Our focus for 2022 was the completion of a strategic review of mining and processing potential at Browns Range. In parallel to this, we continued our ongoing investment in exploration to expand the footprint of known mineralisation and Mineral Resources at Browns Range. While the feasibility study for a full scale beneficiation plant was paused to allow the strategic review, it remained central to our plans in 2022 and progression of the study represents a significant part of Northern Minerals' 2023 work program.

Having built up the Company's expertise and intellectual property on production of both xenotime concentrate and rare earth carbonate (REC) through work to date, 2023 will see the Northern Minerals team continue on the journey to full scale operations and commercialisation of the Browns Range Project.

## Pilot Plant operations

During the financial year 2022, the Pilot Plant milled 8,493 tonnes of ore and produced 70,307kg of Rare Earth Carbonate (REC) containing 6,335kg of dysprosium oxide and 2,140kg of terbium oxide.

At year end, Northern Minerals' total production of REC export product from its Browns Range Pilot Plant since inception reached 281,416 kg, containing 139,852 kg of Rare Earth Oxides, which includes 16,086 kg of dysprosium oxide and 2,140 kg of terbium oxide.

After completion of the test work program in the March quarter, the Browns Range Pilot Plant was placed on care and maintenance as planned. The results of the program are now being used as the foundation for the feasibility study to develop a commercial scale mining and processing operation at Browns Range.

The pilot plant project was a three-year research and development project, aimed at assessing the economic and technical aspects of a heavy rare earth mine and processing facility, and provided valuable information to feed into the current study.

Using existing run-of-mine ore through the Browns Range pilot plant, including the ore sorting circuit, the company undertook large scale test work using different ore from existing stockpiles mined from two open pits at Browns Range.

This important data will be pivotal in designing the proposed circuit and specifications for commercial-scale operations at Browns Range and allow delivery of quality concentrate that meets customer specifications.

## Ore sorting

Northern Minerals made significant steps towards increasing processing efficiency at Browns Range through the test work on large bulk ore samples using ore sorting equipment installed in July 2021. By selecting ore and rejecting waste based primarily on x-ray transmission detection, the ore sorting equipment effectively concentrates ore prior to entering the beneficiation circuit and increases the feed grade to the mill.

The continuous running of the plant simulated full scale operations and validated the potential impact of ore sorting to the economics of a future commercial scale beneficiation plant. A variety of ore types were tested, including lower grade stockpiles that were not previously feasible to process through the pilot plant. The potential to add lower grade material to the feed mix will extend the overall life of any future commercial operation.

The high grade total rare earth oxide (TREO) product from ore sorter test work is expected to increase the overall grade entering the beneficiation circuit by up to 60% for the target xenotime concentrate grade of 25% TREO.  The higher feed grade will reduce the $/kg TREO operational cost, as the fixed process operating costs account for ~46% of the overall cost and the TREO units produced will increase.

Processing higher grade feed can potentially reduce power consumption at Brown's Range, resulting from less tonnes milled for same TREO in xenotime concentrate, and therefore reduce reagent consumption and carbon emissions as well as lowering overall processing costs.

A further potential outcome will be maximising the future beneficiation plant's throughput rate to take advantage of economies of scale, balanced with the available Mineral Resource base and practical mining rates.

Results from this bulk ore sorting test work will be considered in the feasibility study to produce a heavy rare earth xenotime concentrate from a commercialscale beneficiation plant at Browns Range.

## Strategy Review

In the March quarter, Northern Minerals undertook a review of the company's commercialisation strategy. The review confirmed that producing and marketing a mixed heavy rare earth concentrate from the proposed commercial-scale beneficiation plant at Browns Range is the preferred near-term strategy.

The emergence of local customers with capacity and interest in an off-take agreement to purchase the mixed heavy rare earth concentrate feedstock from Browns Range underpins the strategy.

A full scale beneficiation plant at Browns Range represents an accelerated pathway to commercial scale production and is a lower risk approach compared to pursuing a full-scale version of the Browns Range Pilot Plant, which includes both the beneficiation and hydrometallurgical processing circuits.

Developing the beneficiation plant first could potentially deliver cashflow to the Company at least two years earlier than a process plant that includes the hydrometallurgical circuit.

Subsequent to the review, a set of work programs were developed to address risk areas identified during the strategic review. These programs will be part of the feasibility study for a proposed commercial-scale beneficiation plant at Browns Range.

## Feasibility Study

Work on the feasibility study for the full scale beneficiation plant at Browns Range was paused and reviewed as part of the strategic review undertaken in 2022. Work programs were slowed down to ensure the Company's resources are focused on supporting the right strategy.

The proposed beneficiation plant will produce a dysprosium and terbium-rich HREE concentrate product. Producing a concentrate at commercial scale as a first step will allow Northern Minerals to become more competitive and financially resilient.

Since the strategic review, Northern Minerals has been focused on translating the considerable intellectual property obtained through research and development activities into the feasibility study.

## Research &amp; Development (R&amp;D)

The Company lodged its tax return and refundable Research and Development (R&amp;D) tax offset claim with the Australia Taxation Office for the 30 June 2022 financial year. The Company's tax offset claim for the financial year 2022 is for $4.1 million of eligible R&amp;D work.

In financial year 2022, experiments were conducted on all areas of the plant, including ore sorting, fine grinding, magnetic separation, flotation, sulphation bake, leaching, purification, ion exchange, rare earth precipitation and wastewater treatment.

The major focus was on continuing testing the ore sorter on ore from the Wolverine and Gambit West deposits with additional ore sorting tests done on ore from the Banshee deposit.

Significant research effort was also put into testing the behaviour of the ore sorted material being processed in the beneficiation plant and subsequently in the hydrometallurgical plant.

The bulk ore sorting test work is a key input for the full-scale beneficiation plant feasibility study which will also leverage the substantial technical,

operational and economic data from the R&amp;D test work at the Pilot Plant since 2018.

Work also continued on developing an alternative technology for the sulphation baking process to replace the conventional rotary kiln process.

## Off-take and Market

Over 2021-22, Northern Minerals sold over 106 tonnes of heavy rare earth carbonate from the Browns Range Pilot Plant.

Market opportunities have been identified to produce and sell a HREE concentrate from a commercial beneficiation plant. Concentrate from Browns Range has the potential to support the broader critical minerals supply chain domestically as well as enable new export relationships with allied countries looking to partner with Australia to develop alternative supply chains.

Heavy rare earth pricing has experienced significant increases over the last two years - Dysprosium (Dy) prices have increased 39% from July 2020 to a June 2022 price of US$375/kg, while Terbium (Tb) prices have increased 226% over the same period to a current price US$2,135/kg. Yttrium (Y) has risen even more experiencing a 331% increase to US$12.50/kg.

## Dysprosium and terbium demand

The work done over the last 10 years has positioned Northern Minerals to prosper as a key part of the supply chain for the growing permanent magnet industry. Browns Range will produce critical metals for a low carbon future, where rare earth-based permanent magnet electric motors are going to increasingly power the way the world works, from vehicles, to homes, and to industry.

NdFeB permanent magnets used in electric drivetrains in passenger cars require both dysprosium (Dy) and terbium (Tb), the primary payable commodities from Browns Range production, and forecast demand is strong.

Global light vehicle production is forecast to increase significantly by 2030 with more than half of expected to be electric, full hybrid and mild hybrid electric vehicles. By 2030, the global light electric vehicle market is forecast to be dominated by permanent magnet motor powertrains.

The outlook for rare earths, and especially for magnet metals is very positive. Growing adoption of EVs, wind turbines and energy-efficient HVAC systems is driving increased demand for NdFeB permanent magnets.

Governments are adopting policies to reduce carbon intensity, and this is stimulating product innovation to improve energy efficiency. A leading driver for growth in demand will be the rapid uptake of large scale onshore and offshore wind turbines powered by large scale permanent magnets.

Another key driver will be the growth in demand in the electric vehicle industry as well as energy-saving elevators in energy-efficient buildings and growing use of robots in smart manufacturing designs.

This demand from industry is colliding with increased efforts by various governments to boost strategic defence capabilities, a trend only intensified by geopolitical developments.

However, rare earths used in drivetrains have been classified as 'critical minerals' and global supply for HREE is limited due to the monopoly position of Chinese production. The forecast increase in electric vehicle sales and growth is at a risk because of restricted access and supply issues of raw and finished commodities - particularly HREE.

## Northern Territory Exploration

## PRINCIPAL ACTIVITIES

The principal activity of the Company during the course of the financial year was the operation of a pilot scale  project  aimed  at  assessing  the  technical  and  economic  feasibility  of  a  full-scale  commercial operation at its Browns Range Pilot Plant Project. The pilot plant was placed in care and maintenance in the March quarter of 2022.

## REVIEW OF OPERATIONS

During the year, the Company continued operation at the Browns Range Pilot Plant Project. The pilot plant project was a three-year research and development project, to assess the economic and technical feasibility of a heavy rare earth mine and process, and forms part of a broader ongoing feasibility study to develop a commercial scale operation at Browns Range. The plant entered care and maintenance in the March quarter of the financial year after the completion of testwork.

## Browns Range Pilot Plant Operations

Northern Minerals successfully completed the installation and commissioning of the ore sorting system at the end of the 30 June 2021 financial year. During this period, the Group focussed on the testing of the ore sorter system and producing ore sorted material. This material was then processed through the pilot beneficiation plant at Browns Range and converted to a ~30% TREO (Total Rare Earth Oxide) xenotime concentrate, which was then processed through the hydrometallurgical plant to produce a rare earth carbonate (REC). A total of 106,106 kg of REC was shipped for sale to thyssenkrupp.

The Group completed the three year test program on the Pilot Plant at Browns Range during the March 2022 quarter. The results of these test programs including results from the ore sorter will be utilised when the feasibility study is restarted. The Pilot Plant was put on care and maintenance at completion of this testwork.

Following the appointment of a new Chairman and Director in the period the Group undertook a review of concentrate from the proposed commercial-scale beneficiation plant at Browns Range is the preferred near-term strategy for the Company.

As a result of the strategic review, a pause and review of the feasibility study for a full-scale beneficiation plant at Browns Range was implemented post 31 December 2021. A set of work programs have been developed to address risk areas identified during the strategic review which, together with continuing discussions in relation to off-take agreement for the concentrate, will be required prior to restarting the Feasibility Study (FS) for a commercial-scale beneficiation plant at Browns Range.

## EXPLORATION

During the second half of the 2021 calendar year, Northern Minerals conducted a drilling programme at Browns Range completing 17,500 metres of reverse circulation (RC) drilling and 3,200 metres of diamond drilling.

RC drilling targeted anomalous prospects within a 20km radius of the Browns Range pilot processing facility whilst diamond drilling provided core samples from the Wolverine hanging wall for geotechnical and underground mine design purposes.

## Lease terms

In determining the lease terms  for right-of-use-assets,  management  considers  all  facts  and circumstances that create an economic incentive to exercise an extension option, or not exercise a termination option. Extension options (or periods after termination options) are only included in the lease term if the lease is reasonably certain to be extended (or not terminated). For leases of offices, the following factors are normally the most relevant:

- If any leasehold improvements are expected to have a significant remaining value, the Group is typically reasonably certain to extend (or not terminate).
- If  there  are  significant  penalty payments to terminate (or not extend), the  Group is typically reasonably certain to extend (or not terminate).

The lease term is reassessed if an option is actually exercised (or not exercised) or the group becomes obliged to exercise (or not exercise) it. Interest income is recognised as interest accrues using the effective interest method. ## (g) Accounting classification and fair value

## Financial assets

A  financial  asset  shall  be  measured  at  amortised  cost  if  it  is  held  within  a  business  model  whose objective is to hold assets in order to collect contractual cashflows which arise on specified dates and that are solely principal and interest. A debt investment shall be measured at fair value through other comprehensive income if it is held within a business model whose objective is to hold both assets in order to collect contractual cashflows which arise on specified dates that are solely principal and interest as well as selling the asset on the basis of its fair value. Financial assets not measured at amortised cost or at fair value through other comprehensive income are classified as financial assets at fair value through profit or loss. Typically, such financial assets with be either:

- held for  trading,  where  they  are acquired  for  the  purpose  of  selling  in  the  short-term  with  an intention of making a profit, or a derivative; or
- designated as such upon initial recognition where permitted.

Fair value movements are recognised in profit or loss. Despite these requirements, a financial asset may be irrevocably designated as measured at fair value through profit or loss to reduce the effect of, or eliminate, an accounting mismatch.

Financial assets are derecognised when the rights to receive cash flows have expired or have been transferred and the Group has transferred substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership. When there is no reasonable expectation of recovering part or all of a financial asset, its carrying value is written off.

## Impairment of financial assets

The Group recognises a loss allowance for expected credit losses on financial assets which are either measured at amortised cost or fair value through other comprehensive income. The measurement of the loss allowance depends upon the assessment at the end of each reporting period as to whether the financial instruments credit risk has increased significantly since initial recognition, based on reasonable and supportable information that is available, without undue cost or effort to obtain.

Where there has not been a significant increase in exposure to credit risk since initial recognition, a 12month expected credit loss allowance is estimated. This represents a portion of the assets lifetime expected credit losses that is attributable to a default event that is possible within the next 12 months. Where a financial asset has become credit  impaired or where  it  is  determined  that  credit  risk  has increased significantly, the loss allowance is based on the assets lifetime expected credit losses. The amount of expected credit loss recognised is measured on the basis of the probability weighted present value of anticipated cash shortfalls over the life of the instrument discounted at the original effective interest rate.

For financial assets measured at fair value through other comprehensive income, the loss allowance is recognised within other comprehensive income. In all other cases, the loss allowance is recognised in profit or loss.

## Financial liabilities

Financial liabilities other than derivatives are initially recognised at fair value of consideration received net of  transaction costs  as appropriate and are subsequently carried at amortised cost. The  Group derecognises a financial liability when its contractual obligations are discharged or cancelled, or expire.

Amortised cost is calculated by taking into account any discount or premium on acquisition and fees or costs that are an integral part of the effective interest rate. The amortisation is included in finance costs in the statement of profit or loss.

## Property, plant and equipment

Plant and equipment is stated at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment charges. Cost is the fair value of consideration given to acquire the asset at the time of its acquisition or construction and includes the direct cost of bringing the asset to the location and condition necessary for operation and its estimated future cost of closure and rehabilitation.

Any item of property, plant and equipment is derecognised upon disposal or when no further economic benefits are expected from its use or disposal.

(i) The grant date fair value of the award.

(ii) The expired portion of the vesting period.

The charge to the statement of profit or loss for the period is the cumulative amount as calculated above less the amounts already charged in previous periods. There is a corresponding entry to equity.

## (iii) Valuation of Options, Performance Rights and Share Plan Shares

The fair value of the equity-settled share options granted to third parties is estimated as at the date of grant using the Black-Scholes option pricing model taking into account the terms and conditions upon which the options and shares were granted.

The  initial  undiscounted  value  of  the  performance  rights  issued  under  the  Employee  Performance Rights Plan is the value of an underlying share in the Company as traded on ASX at the date of deemed date  of  grant  of  the  performance  right.    As  the  performance  conditions  are  not  market  based performance conditions, no discount is applied.

The assessed fair value at grant date of the performance rights issued on  21 December 2021 was independently determined using an adjusted form of the Black-Scholes Model which includes a Monte Carlo simulation model that takes into account the exercise price, the vesting period, the share price at grant date and expected price volatility of the underlying share, the expected dividend yield, the riskfree  interest  rate  for  the  term  of  the  right  and  the  correlations  and  volatilities  of  the  peer  group companies.

The expected life of the options is based on historical data and is not necessarily indicative of exercise patterns that may occur. The expected volatility reflects the assumption that the historical volatility is indicative of future trends, which may also not necessarily be the actual outcome. No other features of options granted were incorporated into the measurement of fair value.

The fair value of options, performance rights and share plan shares are recognised as an expense over the period from grant to vesting date.
