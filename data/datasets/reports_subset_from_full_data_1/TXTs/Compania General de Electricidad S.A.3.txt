## I. MACROECONOMIC CONTEXT FOR THE YEAR 2022

## Macroeconomic context

The Company carries out its operations mainly in Argentina, so business depends to a great extent on the evolution of the main variables of the macroeconomic context of the country, which have been highly volatile,  particularly  the  tariff  policy  measures  adopted  by  the National  Government,  the  local  social  and  political  conditions,  and  the  international scenario.

At the beginning of the year, although the COVID 19 health risk subsided, limiting the level of activity and inflation until mid-2021, Russia's invasion of Ukraine broke out, with very harmful consequences both in humanitarian and economic terms.

In economic terms, the war led to a typical oil supply shock that initially caused oil prices to rise from around USD70/80 per barrel (WTI) to highs of USD 125 in March. Throughout the second quarter, the price of oil remained oscillating between 100 and 120 dollars per barrel, but affected the rest of the commodities markets, both energy (such as the price of  liquefied  gas)  and  agricultural  commodities.  Between  March  and  April,  the  price  of soybean rose from 600 to 620 dollars per ton, wheat from 343 to 390 and corn from 253 to 298, among the most relevant commodities.

From May onwards, with  the  war  entering  undefined  territory,  commodity  prices  were tempered in terms of value and volatility. The price of oil stabilized at around USD/Brl 85 (WTI, that of soybeans oscillated around USD/Tn 530, wheat USD/Tn 300 and corn USD/Tn 240).

The impact of this shock on the level of activity was most clearly felt in some European economies,  the  most  dependent  on  gas  supplies  from  Russia.  For  example,  Germany, according to preliminary information, would be averaging a growth of only 1% in 2022. In the rest of the world, a slowdown in growth was noted, which has to do with the impact of the  war,  but  also  with  monetary  factors,  the  increase  in  interest  rates  and  persistent bottlenecks in several sectors of goods and inputs (derived from the sanitary restrictions imposed by China).

While, in 2021, the main world economies grew uninterruptedly, managing to recover a large part of the GDP declines that occurred in 2020, a generalized slowdown was noted in 2022,  without  signifying  a  recessionary  environment.  GDP  growth  rates  in  2022  are estimated at 2.0% in the USA, 1.0% in Germany, 2.5% in France, 4.0% in the UK, 3.5% in Italy, 5.0% in Spain, 2.5% in Brazil, 2.5% in Mexico and 2.0% in Chile.

The impact of the commodity price war, combined with external demand at elevated levels, resulted in an increase in global inflation that went beyond the jump recorded in 2021. Between June and December 2021, U.S. inflation had risen from 1.4% to 6.8% annually, in Germany from 0.4% to 5.2%, in China from 0.2% to 2.3%, in Mexico from 3.2% to 7.4%, in Brazil from 4.5% to 10.7%. In 2022, this process worsened, driving additional increases in inflation, which in the case of the USA reached 7.1% per year, in Germany 10% per year, in Mexico 7.8% per year, and in Brazil 5.9% per year (data as of November 2022).

The main Central Banks reacted aggressively to the overheating of economies. Until the beginning of 2022, the Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank diagnosed that the increase in inflation was explained by mostly transitory factors such as the increase in the international price of oil, the rest of the energy inputs or metals (prior to the war in Ukraine)

and also by the resettlement of relative prices (such as tourism, passenger transportation or entertainment), as well as by bottlenecks in the production of intermediate inputs and final goods.

However, this diagnosis and the plans foreseen for its monetary policies were left under review. The war in Ukraine and the March 2022 commodity price hike finalized a different macroeconomic picture and economic policy priorities.

The US Federal Reserve was forced to accelerate the monetary tightening it had announced in  2021.  In  March,  it  announced  a  schedule  for  reducing  its  assets  and  consequently injecting less liquidity into the financial system by selling or not renewing maturities of Treasury bonds and mortgage assets. Since then, the assets on the Fed's balance sheet were reduced by almost USD 400.000 million from USD 8.9 billion in March to USD 8.5 million in December.

Simultaneously, it recalibrated the interest rate adjustment path. The first increase in the Fed Funds rate was in March 2022 and amounted to 25 basis points (bps), to raise it from 0.25% to 0.5%. This was followed by a 50-bps hike in May, 4 hikes of 75 bps in June, July, September, November and a final 50 bps hike in December, leaving the interest rate in the range of 4.25-4.50%.

The rest of the large Central Banks such as the European Central Bank (ECB), the Bank of England and the Bank of Japan (BOJ) followed the Fed's lead. The ECB raised the interest rate 250 bps from 0% in June to 2.5% in December, the Bank of England raised the interest rate from 0.25% to 3.5% while the BOJ has not yet decided on any move.

The  war  in  Ukraine  and  monetary  tightening  led  to  volatile  financial  markets  with corrections in almost all asset classes, corporate bonds, sovereigns, stock markets and currencies. In the last twelve months, up to December 2022, the Dow Jones and Nasdaq indices fell 8% and 32% respectively. In line with the Fed's actions, interest rates on both short and long-term Treasury bonds strengthened (4.3% for the 3-month bond and 3.75% for the 10-year bond) while credit spreads of the riskiest companies (High Yield) widened from levels of 310 to 461 basis points. In the same sense, the credit risk of emerging bonds increased from 370 basis points to 420 (EMBI +, JP Morgan). In the case of international commodity prices, among the main markets, the price of oil rose 4.3% (from USD75.2 to USD 78.5 per barrel WTI), the price of copper fell 14% and soybeans rose 10.5% (from USD 492 to USD 544 per ton).

Another event with a possible economic impact on the region was the Brazilian presidential election, where the coalition led by Luis Ignacio Da Silva defeated Jair Bolsonaro's ruling party in the second round. Da Silva took office in January 2023. Judging by the reaction of the financial markets, a 180° turn in terms of economic policy is not expected, expecting the economic fundamentals of the last thirty years to be maintained. The value of the real remained at levels close to 5.3 to the dollar, the Bovespa stock market index at around 110,000 points, and the EMBI country risk close to historic lows of 260 bps.

## Argentina's economic performance 2022

The most important local economic event at the beginning of the year was the agreement reached with the International Monetary Fund, which cleared the debt maturity horizon with  the  organization,  in  exchange  for  a  program  that  includes  quantitative  fiscal  and monetary  targets.  The  primary  deficit  originally  set  for  2022  was  $  1.8  billions,  an adjustment with respect to 2021 of around 0.5 percentage points of GDP. The signing of the Agreement coincided with a change in the international context that had a contrasting impact  on  public  finances.  The  conflict  in  Ukraine  led  to  an  escalation  of  international commodity prices, among them, those that Argentina exports (soybeans, grains) but also those that it imports (energy). Thus, while tax collection accelerated because of higher revenues from export taxes, spending was accompanied by higher payments of energy subsidies.

The net impact was negative. Central government primary spending grew 10 points above revenues in the first half of the year (75% annual versus 65% annual, respectively). The fiscal  target  for  the  first  quarter  was  barely  met  thanks  to  the  inclusion  of  non-cash generating  property  rents  (reluctantly  accepted  by  the  IMF),  while  the  second  quarter target,  which  had  to  be  substantially  revised  upwards  after  correcting  the  program's inflation assumptions, was also barely met and after spending was stepped on.

With respect to the level of activity, a high growth rate was maintained throughout 2022. According to the official information released up to October/November, GDP would average a growth rate slightly above 5.0% in 2022. This dynamic was driven by the goods and services sectors. Industry and construction are targeting 5% growth in 2022, trade 6% and tourism 35%. The latter sector still compares against levels where restrictions to the movement of people derived from the 2020-21 health crisis persisted. Contrasting with these sectors are Agriculture and Livestock with projected growth of less than 2%, and Banking and Insurance with projected growth of 1%.

In line with the level of activity, the labor market also recovered. According to the latest published information, the activity rate was 47.9% in the third quarter of 2022 versus 46.9% in the fourth quarter of 2021, the employment rate was 44.2% versus 43.6% in the fourth quarter of 2021, and the unemployment rate was 7.1% versus 7.1% in the fourth quarter of 2021.

In the first eleven months of the year, exports increased 15.3% (USD 10,946 M) driven by a 17.0% improvement in prices, while quantities decreased -1.4%. However, the trade surplus narrowed substantially due to the strong growth in imports, driven by high fuel prices,  but  also  due  to  the  increase  in  imported  quantities.  Between  January  and November, imports increased 34.3% (USD 19,525 M) with respect to the same period of the previous year, with an increase of 16.7% in prices and 15.0% in quantities. Thus, while in the first eleven months of 2021 the trade surplus amounted to USD 14,379 M, in the same period of 2022 it was reduced to USD 5,800 M.

Between  January  and  November  2022,  exporters  recorded  receipts  from  collections  of goods exports for about USD 82,760 million, while shipments totaled USD 82,100 million, so a slight increase in the external debt for advances and pre-financing of goods of about USD 670 million is estimated for this period. Not all sectors evolved in the same way.

In  September,  through  the  Export  Increase  Program,  the  government  established  a differential exchange rate of $ 200 per ton of soybeans and its derivatives (30% higher than the official exchange rate), effective only for that month. As a result, in September the oilseeds and grains sector earned foreign currency for USD 5,735 M above the exported value, indicating that there was an increase in trade financing. This amount was partially reversed between October and November, in response to the partial cancellation of the September indebtedness. In December the government reestablished (also temporarily) the special dollar for soybeans, setting it at $ 230 per ton. It is estimated that during the year the oilseeds and grains sector will earn some USD 1,500 / 2,000 million above the values shipped. As for the rest of the sectors, foreign currency inflows in the first eleven months of the year were lower than the values shipped, which indicates that commercial indebtedness has been reduced.

On the other hand, it is estimated that in the first eleven months of the year, the debt for imports of goods would have increased by around USD 9,000 M. Thus, the ratio between foreign indebtedness and the level of imports increased to 37.6% in November 2022.

The evolution of trade in goods and trade debt for exports and imports resulted in a net result for goods in the foreign exchange market of USD 18,600 M in the first eleven months of the year, showing a year-on-year increase of about USD 4,300 M. This amount was higher by about USD 7,800 M than the result of the FOB trade balance for the same period, which recorded a year-on-year fall of USD 7,050 M.

Regarding the private sector financial debt, as of the renegotiations recorded in the first eleven  months  of  2022,  net  payments  made  through  the  foreign  exchange  market represented 27% of the original maturities, impacting on lower net purchases for about USD 2,254 million.

As for the BCRA's performance in the foreign exchange market, net purchases for USD 5,824 million were recorded in the year-to-date, while International Reserves increased by USD 4,935 million. The variation in International Reserves was explained by the decrease in the valuation of assets for about USD 2,200 M, due to sales in the Local Currency System for about USD 780 M. These movements were partially offset by the net income of foreign currency debt of the National Government for about USD 2,200 M (net income from the IMF for about USD 5,166 M and net payments of other foreign currency debt for USD 3,000 M) and the net purchases of the BCRA in the exchange market for the aforementioned USD 5,824 M.

Until August, the inflation rate accelerated. The third quarter ended with a monthly average inflation of 6.8% (+1.3 p.p. with respect to the second quarter of 2022) while in the fourth quarter it decelerated to 5.4% monthly (-1.4 p.p. with respect to the third quarter of 2022). In November, the monthly inflation rate slowed to 4.9%, the lowest level since February of  this  year,  and  in  December  it  was  5.1%.  This  moderation  followed  the  significant acceleration of the general price level in July (7.4%) and August (7.0%), in a context of greater  local  financial  volatility,  exchange  rate  uncertainty  and  increased  inflation expectations. The year-on-year inflation rate continued to rise during the last months and closed the year at 94.8% annually.

After  detaching  itself  from  the  evolution  of  retail  prices  for  some  months,  the  BCRA accelerated  the  "crawling-peg":  until  September,  the  monthly  devaluation  rate  lagged inflation, a situation that was reversed as of October. However, this was not enough: with the annual devaluation of around 72.5% versus inflation of 94.8% per year, the exchange rate recorded a real appreciation of 12.9% in 2022. Throughout the year, the gap between the official exchange rate and the CCL (cash with liquidation) dollar benchmark was around 100%.

The evolution of the public accounts was marked by the effort to meet the goals of the agreement with the IMF signed in March. The program envisaged reducing the primary deficit from 3% of GDP in 2021 ($ 1.4 billion) to 2.5% ($ 1.8 billion) in 2022, on the basis of strengthening revenues and controlling spending, the latter focused on saving energy subsidies due to the tariff segmentation that was expected to be implemented.

The war in Ukraine and the inflationary acceleration itself disrupted the plans: on the one hand, revenues rose more than expected due to the dynamics of prices, both local and international  (energy  and  food);  on  the  other  hand,  expenses  also  accelerated  due  to higher spending on energy subsidies. The IMF modified the annual fiscal target due to the higher expected inflation, raising the primary deficit to almost $ 2.1 billion, a target that will be met thanks to the liquefaction of spending due to the acceleration of inflation (a liquefaction  that  was  not  offset  by  the  payment  of  various  bonuses  and  food  aid  to beneficiaries of minimum pensions and social plans) and to additional sources of income (the "soy dollar" I and II in September and December, which increased the collection of withholding  taxes  on  exports  in  those  months,  as  well  as  the  advance  payment  of  an income tax advance corresponding to 2023 for companies with extraordinary profits).

The financing of the Treasury's needs was mainly supported by the BCRA, both in pesos and dollars.  Peso  debt  payments  followed  a  meandering  path.  During  the  first  quarter 2022, the dynamics observed in 2021 continued, with the Treasury achieving positive net financing (placements more than total maturities), but the dynamics changed in the second quarter with accelerating inflation and market volatility. The BCRA was forced to intervene by buying more than $ 1 trillion of debt in the secondary market to support prices and decompress maturities. Thus, in the second quarter of 2022, the debt rollover rate, which had been comfortably above 130% on average at the beginning of the year, ended at 98%, and only thanks to this help from the BCRA. Debt maturities in dollars continued to be paid with  reserves  (interest  on  foreign  currency  bonds  and  loans  from  agencies),  while payments to the IMF were made using the disbursements corresponding to the signing of the program and to the fulfillment of each quarter's goals.

The BCRA's monetary assistance to the National Treasury through the Temporary Advances remained within the limits established in the Extended Facilities Program (EFP) agreed with the IMF. Likewise, the National Treasury fulfilled its commitment of not using the remaining balance of temporary advances in the last part of the year. Thus, the accumulated primary expansion of the public sector in terms of GDP was among the lowest in recent years, representing  only  0.8%  of  GDP.  The  foreign  currency  purchase  operations  from  the soybean  complex  carried  out  within  the  framework  of  the  "Export  Increase  Program" implied a considerable expansion of liquidity during September and, to a lesser extent, in December. Another factor that implied an expansion of liquidity during the period under analysis were the purchases of Treasury securities in the secondary debt market in pesos, which were resumed after months without BCRA's participation. Liquidity expansion was ultimately  channeled  to  monetary  regulation  instruments.  Because  of  the  above,  the second half of the year saw an increase in the balance of interest-bearing liabilities. In 2022, the BCRA's interest-bearing liabilities accrued interest for $ 3,386 M, while the stock increased by $ 4,975 M.

The stock of interest-bearing liabilities of the BCRA will reach a value of 8% of GDP (annual average) in 2022.

Throughout the year, as monthly inflation accelerated, the BCRA raised the monetary policy interest rate, which by the last quarter of the year was consolidating in positive territory in real terms. In the same way, the minimum guaranteed interest rate for time deposits was also raised.

As of December 2022, total peso deposits grew 90.3% annually (4.5 percentage points less than inflation). Encouraged by the positive real interest rate, time deposits grew 102% annually as of December. Transactional deposits (checking and savings accounts) grew 73.9% year-on-year.  The  monetary  aggregates  are  at  a  value  close  to  their  historical average (2007-November 2022), while the circulating money held by the public was 41% below the historical average in November, one of the lowest records of the last 15 years.

## Hydrocarbon activity in Argentina

During  2022,  domestic  hydrocarbon  production  continued  to  increase  because  of  the recovery of post-pandemic demand. Thus, with year-on-year increases of 13.5% in oil and almost 7% in gas, production levels not seen since 2009-2010 were reached. The Neuquina Basin was once again fundamental to obtain these records. The production of unconventional oil and gas increased 23% and 48%, respectively, and, on average, they already represent half of all the production obtained in the country.

In addition to domestic demand, the expansion projects of the Valley Pipeline System and the  reactivation  of  the  Trans-Andean  Pipeline  underpin  the  growth  possibilities  of  oil exports. In this sense, the volume exported of domestic hydrocarbons increased 43% last year, generating revenues of more than USD 5 billion.

The construction of the Néstor Kirchner gas pipeline, the first section of which is expected to be inaugurated in mid-2023, represents an optimistic outlook for the gas sector since it will make it possible to increase the production of the Neuquina Basin fields, which currently have a ceiling in their transportation capacity, while improving the supply of residential consumption, reducing dependence on energy imports and promoting investments for the expansion of the petrochemical complex in Bahía Blanca and the development of projects for the construction of infrastructure for exports.

With the purpose of enabling investments to promote production in all the country's basins and satisfy hydrocarbon needs, as a complement, extension and empowerment of Decree N° 892/2020 "Plan for the Promotion of Argentine Natural Gas Production - Supply and Demand Scheme 2020-2024" and the results derived therefrom, on November 3, 2022, Decree N° 770/2022 "Plan for the Reinsurance and Enhancement of Federal Hydrocarbon Production, Domestic Self-Supply, Exports, Substitution of Imports and Expansion of the Transportation System for all hydrocarbon basins in the country 2023-2028" was published in the Official Gazette.  The purpose of this decree was (i) to grant predictability to the filling of the Nestor Kirchner gas pipeline from the Neuquina Basin, which in its first stage would be operative in June 2023 (ii)  to  extend  in  time  and  in  advance  until  2028  the contracting of those volumes assigned in Decree 892/2020, and (iii) to extend in time and in  advance  until  2028  the  contracting  of  those  volumes  assigned  in  Decree  892/2020, which would be operative in June 2023. in Decree 892/2020 and its amendments (iii) to promote the production of all those basins that have idle transportation capacity and that can immediately cooperate in the substitution of alternative fuels that are more expensive for the system ("Incremental Activity Plan"). ## III CGC INVESTMENTS IN OTHER COMPANIES

CGC  is  a  leading  energy  company  operating  in  Argentina  and  is  engaged  in  the development, exploration and production of gas, oil and, to a lesser extent, LPG (upstream business). ## Regulatory Aspects

TGN is the holder of a license to provide public natural gas transportation services, by virtue of which it is granted the exclusive right to operate the two gas pipelines it owns in the  North  and  Center-West  regions  of  Argentina. ## Effects  of  the  economic  emergency  on  the  License.  The  Comprehensive  Tariff  Review ("RTI")

The License was subject to renegotiation by virtue of the Public Emergency Law N° 25,561 enacted in January 2002 ("PEL"), which also provided for the pesification of natural gas transportation tariffs for the local market and the repeal of the semiannual adjustment mechanism based on the Producer Price Index. Within such legal framework, and after more than thirteen years of tariff  freezing,  in  March  2017  TGN  entered  into  a  License Readjustment  Agreement  with  the  PEN  (the  "Comprehensive  Agreement")  which  was

## Compañía General de Combustibles S.A. Annual report 2022

ratified and entered into force with the issuance of Decree N° 251 of March 27, 2018. This concluded  the  renegotiation  process  developed  under  the  PEL.  The  provisions  of  the Comprehensive Agreement cover the contractual period between January 6, 2002, and the date of termination of the License.

Between April 2014 and December 2017 TGN received successive transitory tariff increases on account of the Comprehensive Tariff Review ("RTI") carried out by ENARGAS as from March 2016.

The  regulatory  framework  of  the  industry  establishes  the  application  of  non-automatic semiannual tariff review mechanisms due to the variations observed in the prices of the economy linked to the costs of the service, to maintain the economic-financial sustainability of the provision and the quality of the service rendered.

In September 2019, the Energy Secretariat issued Resolution N° 521/2019 (as amended by Resolution N° 751/2019) deferring the semi-annual tariff adjustment that should have been applied as from October 1, 2019, until February 1, 2020.

On June 1, 2021, TGN was notified of certain resolutions issued by both the Ministry of Economy ("MECON") as well as ENARGAS and validated by a presidential decree, by virtue of which a Transitional Tariff Regime ("RTT") was established.

The RTT implies: (i) that TGN's tariffs will continue to be frozen, (ii) that the company must continue to provide gas transportation services, (iii) the prohibition to distribute dividends, prepay loans with shareholders and acquire companies or grant credits (except to users or contractors  that  are  not  shareholders  of  TGN)  except  with  prior  authorization  from ENARGAS, and (iv) that during the RTT no mandatory investment plan applies. Also, the RTT considers the possibility of ENARGAS adjusting TGN's tariffs as from April 1, 2022.

On June 30, 2021, TGN filed an administrative appeal against Decree N° 1020/20, Joint Resolution N° 2/21 of the MECON and ENARGAS which approved the RTT ad referendum of  the  PEN,  Decree  N°  353/21  which  ratified  Joint  Resolution  N°  2,  and  Resolution  N° 150/21 of ENARGAS through which the RTT tariff charts were put into effect. Based on the express wording of the Gas Law (Article 48) and the "Basic Rules of the Transportation License" (Section 9.8), TGN considers that the cost of any subsidy to natural gas users should be borne by the National Treasury and not by TGN or, failing that, TGN should be compensated by the PEN. Likewise, TGN considers that neither the PEN, the MECON and/or the ENARGAS have the power to establish the prohibitions to do, imposed by the RTT.

By Resolution N° 518/2021 dated December 27, 2021, ENARGAS called for a new public hearing which was held on January 19, 2022, during which the gas transportation and distribution licensees presented their proposals for the transitory tariff adjustment for the year 2022.

On February 18, 2022, TGN entered into a transitory agreement with the MECON and ENARGAS that established a 60% increase in transportation tariffs as from March 2022 (the  "2022  Transitory  Agreement").  Such  agreement,  which  will  remain  in  force  until December  2022  unless  extended  by  agreement  of  the  parties,  does  not  contemplate mandatory investments, but establishes: (i) that the company must continue providing gas transportation  services,  (ii)  the  prohibition  to  distribute  dividends,  prepay  loans  with shareholders and acquire companies or grant credits (except to users or contractors that are not shareholders of TGN) except with prior authorization of ENARGAS and (iii) that during  its  term,  TGN  and  its  controlling  shareholder  Gasinvest  S.  A.  ("Gasinvest") undertake not to initiate actions or claims against the National Government based on the tariff freeze provided by the Solidarity Law.

The  Transitional  Agreement  2022  became  effective  on  February  22,  2022,  as  from  its ratification by PEN Decree N° 91/22 and by ENARGAS Resolution N° 59/22 dated February 23, 2022, which approved the transitional tariff charts as from March 1, 2022.

On December 6, 2022 the PEN issued the Necessity and Urgency Decree N° 815/22 by which (i) extends for one year the term to conclude the renegotiation of the RTI within the framework  of  the  provisions  of  Section  5  of  the  Solidarity  Law,  and  (ii)  extends  the intervention of ENARGAS, for a term of one year or until the new tariff charts resulting from the renegotiation of the RTI enter into force, whichever occurs first. It also instructs ENARGAS to carry out the necessary measures to tend to a transitional tariff adjustment, in accordance with the provisions of Decree N° 1020/20.

In this sense, by means of Resolution N° 523/22 of December 7, 2022, ENARGAS called for  a  new  public  hearing  which  was  held  on  January  4,  2023,  during  which  the  gas transportation and distribution licensees presented their proposals for a transitional tariff adjustment for the current year.

## TGM

The Company has a direct interest of 15.8% in TGM. The other shareholders of TGM are Tecpetrol Internacional S.L., Central Puerto S.A. and Total Gas y Electricidad Argentina S.A. which own 31.5%, 20.0%, and 32.7% of TGM's capital stock, respectively.

TGM operates the pipeline that transports natural gas from Aldea Brasilera, in the province of Entre Rios in Argentina to Uruguayana, in the state of Rio Grande do Sul in Brazil. The pipeline  has  an  extension  of  approximately  437  km,  a  diameter  of  24  inches  and  a transportation capacity of 15.0 million m3 per day. TGM's operating license expires in 2027 and is extendable for 10 years, subject to review and approval by the National Government.

Because of the crisis in the energy sector caused by a deficit in the supply of natural gas and electricity,  the  Argentine  government  took  a  series  of  measures  in  relation  to  gas exports and the redirection of transportation capacity until mid-2008 when gas exports to the Federative Republic of Brazil ceased completely. This generated a litigation with YPF, related to the breach of the contract for the firm transportation of natural gas for a volume of 2.8 million m3 per day, on "take or pay" conditions signed in September 1998 between YPF and the Company. This contract, which was terminated by TGM in April 2009 due to YPF's default, represented 99.9% of the Company's revenues.

After different legal actions TGM and YPF entered into, in December 2017, a Transactional Agreement that established YPF's obligation to pay TGM as indemnification the sum of USD 107 million in January 2018, plus the sum of USD 7 million in seven annual and equal installments between February 2018 and February 2024, as full and final payment of all arbitration  and  legal  actions  and  claims  that  TGM,  currently  or  eventually,  may  have against YPF by virtue of arbitration awards.

Additionally,  TGM  and  YPF  entered  into  an  interruptible  transportation  export  contract (ITS).

TGM, because of the Transactional Agreement together with the new ITS contract, was able  to  recompose  its  Net  Worth  and  its  Legal  Reserve,  which  allowed  it  to  cancel  its operating debts. Based on the foregoing, it is reasonable to expect that TGM will be able to continue providing the service in a safe manner and meet its operating costs without the need for any type of financing.

For the year ended December 31, 2022, TGM recorded a net loss of $ 214.0 million. TGM's assets amounted to $ 1,798.5 million and shareholders' equity to $ 1,300.0 million.

## Oil Sales - María Inés and Noroeste

In fiscal year 2022, because of the price positioning of María Inés oil in recent years, 100% of sales were destined to the export market.

Unlike previous years where the export sales procedure was through tenders (Tender) and continuing  with  the  strategy  implemented  during  fiscal  year  2020,  the  direct  sales procedure was maintained to optimize each charge and maximize prices, mapping and monitoring the sale of other producers so that buyers could complete their load; in order to look for opportunities in those periods and thus increase the demand interested in María Inés oil. The co-loading operations allowed obtaining very good discounts in the deliveries

of the first half of the year despite the own and current limitations of the loading port of Muelle Pte. Illia.

The price scenario in the local market, the positioning of María Inés oil in the international market and the lack of interest of local refineries in running María Inés crude oil, created an opportunity to continue developing and strengthening our export markets.

## X. ANALYSIS OF RESULTS, FINANCIAL POSITION AND CASH FLOWS

## Results

The results for fiscal year 2022 show a final net income of $ 7,874 million, compared to a final net income for fiscal 2021 of $ 911 million.

EBITDA adjusted for dividends received for fiscal 2022 was $ 43,262 million, a decrease of $  23,435  million  compared  to  fiscal  2021  (see  Note  6  to  the  Consolidated  Financial Statements as of December 31, 2022).

In June 2021, as explained in detail in Note 3 to the Consolidated Financial Statements as of December 31, 2021, CGC acquired from TIPTOP Energy Limited 100% of Sinopec (now CGC Energía S.A.U.). This company owns participations in hydrocarbon exploration and exploitation areas located in the Cuyana and Golfo San Jorge basins.

As  of  July  1,  2021,  the  Company  consolidates  the  operations  of  CGC  Energía  S.A.U. Therefore, in the comparisons of volumes sold of crude oil and gas, it should be considered that sales of CGC Energía S.A. were not consolidated for 435.9 MMm3 and 35.6 MMm3 equivalent of crude oil and gas respectively, for the first half of 2021, which represent approximately 50% of the annual volume sold of CGC Energía S.A.U.

Revenues for the year increased by $ 5,370.9 million, representing 3.8%, from $ 141,920.4 million as of December 31, 2021 to $ 147,291.3 million as of December 31, 2022, mainly as a consequence of the consolidation effect explained in the previous paragraph, an effect partially  offset  by  lower  incentives  (Resolution  N°  46-E/2017)  accrued  in  2022  for  $ 25,775.5.

Revenues from gas sales amounted to $ 43,442.4 and $ 46,736.3 as of December 31, 2022, and 2021, respectively, representing a decrease of $ 3,293.9 (7.0%) with respect to 2021, due to a decrease in average sales prices measured at real value in pesos of 9%. Although in 2021 CGC Energía's sales were only considered as of July 1, 2021, there are no variations because the sale of gas in CGC Energía S.A.U. is not material with respect to consolidated  sales.  Consolidated  gas  sales  volumes  increased  2%  mainly  due  to  the consolidation effect.

Revenues from crude oil sales amounted to $ 97,116.4 and $ 62,724,2 in 2022 and 2021 respectively, representing an increase of $ 34,392,28 (54,8%). When considering CGC Energía S.A.U.'s sales in the consolidated as of July 1, 2021, there has been an increase with  respect  to  the  consolidated  sales  in  2022.  Consolidated  crude  oil  sales  volumes increased by 58% in 2022 compared to 2021, mainly due to the consolidation effect.

The cost  of  revenues  totaled  $  118,652.0  million  in  2022,  representing  an  increase  of 38.1%  compared  to  the  $  85,933.5  million  recorded  in  2021.  The  increase  in  CGC's operating  costs  is  mainly  explained  by  the  variation  in  the  item  "royalties,  fee  and easements" which increased by $ 6,875.8 million compared to the previous year due to the consolidated effect explained in the previous paragraphs and the increase of $ 10,601.8 in contracted services, $ 5,109.6 in well maintenance expenses and $ 6,193.5 for spare parts and repairs generated by the consolidated effect explained in the previous paragraphs and higher operations in fiscal year 2022.

Gross profit for fiscal 2022 amounted to $ 28,639.3 million, $ 27,347.6 million lower than that recorded in fiscal 2021, with margins over revenues of 19.4% and 39.4% for 2022 and 2021, respectively.

Selling expenses totaled $ 7,098.9 million in fiscal year 2022, representing an increase of $ 1,652.4 million or 30.3% compared to the previous year. The increase corresponds to i) a higher gross income tax charge of $ 1,043.7 million due to the increase in invoicing in the domestic market mainly due to the twelve-month consolidation of CGC Energía S.A.U. and ii) a higher dispatch and transportation charge of $ 592.7 million due to the higher activity in fiscal 2022.

## XI. PROSPECTS

2022 was the first year of full operation of the assets in the Golfo San Jorge and Cuyana basins,  acquired  in  2021  through  the  acquisition  of  Sinopec  Argentina  Exploration  and Production Inc. (now CGC Energía S.A.U.).

During this period, the Company has considerably increased its activity in these areas, almost  doubling  the  number  of  pulling  rigs  in  Golfo  San  Jorge  and  introducing  three workover rigs. Additionally, as of June, a first drilling rig was incorporated and a second one was added by the end of the year, drilling 15 new wells after several years without this type of activity.

On the other hand, CGC continued with its investment plan in the Austral Basin, with the main objective of maintaining the production and activity levels achieved during 2021, while exploratory wells were drilled to analyze the estimated potential of the basin and its sustainable development.

2023 is  projected  to  be  a  year  of  strong  investment  and  growth  for  the  Company.  In addition to the development plans in the Golfo San Jorge Basin, the Company plans to significantly increase the activity in the Austral Basin areas, from the award of incremental gas  production  volumes  in  rounds  5.1  and  5.2  corresponding  to  the  "Plan  for  the reinsurance and enhancement of federal hydrocarbon production, domestic self-supply, exports, import substitution and expansion of the transportation system for all hydrocarbon basins in the country 2023-2028" (Decree No. 770/2022).

Such program will allow CGC to rapidly increase its current production level, developing short cycle projects in a more profitable way taking advantage of the market opportunity given by the need for more local production and the high prices of LNG.

With  respect  to  the  macroeconomic  and  political  context,  CGC  expects  to  develop  its activities during the current year under conditions of high uncertainty and possible volatility in the main economic variables. Among the most important factors to consider are the national Open, Simultaneous, and Obligatory Primaries (P.A.S.O.) elections in August, the national  general  elections  in  October  and  a  possible  ballotage  for  the  election  of  the President in November.

In  relation  to  the  previously  mentioned,  the Company will pay special attention to the evolution of the variables related to the stability of the exchange market, inflation, and access  to  financing  sources,  since  abrupt  changes  in  these  variables  may  affect  the execution of the investment plan projected for 2023.

In line with these actions, financing efforts will continue to focus on the optimization of the capital structure, as well as on the search for additional sources of financing, based on the Company's investment objectives and growth.

## Unit of measurement

IAS 29 requires that the Financial Statements of an entity, whose functional currency is that of a high inflation economy, are expressed in terms of the unit of measurement current at the closing date of the reporting year, regardless of whether they are based on the historical cost method or on the current cost method. For this purpose, in general terms, inflation from the date of acquisition or from the date of revaluation, as appropriate, must be computed in non-monetary items. These requirements also apply to comparative information in the Financial Statements.

To determine the existence of a hyperinflationary economy under the terms of IAS 29, the standard details a series of factors to consider, including a cumulative inflation rate over three years that is close to or exceeds 100%. For this reason, in accordance with IAS 29, the Argentine economy must be considered as highly inflationary as from July 1, 2018.

In turn, Law N° 27,468, published in the Official Gazette on December 4, 2018, amended Section 10  of  Law  N°  23,928  and  its  subsequent  amendments,  providing  that  the  repeal  of  the  all regulations that establish or authorize price indexing, monetary restatement, cost variation or any other way of restatement of debts, taxes, prices or tariffs of goods, works or services, does not apply  to  the  Financial  Statements,  to  which  the  provisions  of  Section  62  in  fine  of  General Companies Law N° 19,550 (1984 restated text), as amended, shall continue to apply. ## 2.2.2 Participation in joint arrangements and associates

## 2.2.2.1 Participation in joint arrangements

A joint arrangement is an arrangement of which two or more parties have joint control, i,e, the contractually agreed sharing of control of an arrangement, which exists only when decisions about the relevant activities require the unanimous consent of the parties sharing control.

Under IFRS 11, investments in joint arrangements are either joint operations or joint ventures, depending on the contractual rights and obligations of the parties. The Group has analyzed the nature of Upstream joint arrangements and determined that they fall within the scope of joint operations. ## 2.2.2.2 Associates

Associates  are  all  entities  over  which  the  Group  has  significant  influence,  being  the  power  to participate in the financial and operating policy decisions of the investee, but not control or joint control, (generally accompanying a shareholding of between 20% and 50% of the voting rights).

The Group records its investments in associates using the equity method. Under the equity method, the initial recognition of the investment in an associate is made at cost, including goodwill at the date of acquisition, if applicable. The carrying amount is increased or decreased to recognize the percentage of the Group participation in the profits or losses of the associates and include said result in the Group profits or losses. The investment in the associate will be accounted for applying the  equity  method,  from  the  moment  it  becomes  an  associate.  On  the  acquisition  date,  the difference between the cost of the investment and the portion that corresponds to the investor in the net fair value of the associate's identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities will be recorded in the results for the fiscal year. Therefore: (a) Goodwill related to the associate will be included in the carrying amount of the investment. However, the amortization of said goodwill is not allowed and therefore will not be included in the determination of the investor's participation in the results of the associate. (b) The excess that over the cost of the investment the investor's participation in the net fair value of the associate's identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities will be excluded from the book value of the investment and will be included as income in the determination of participation.

Unrealized gains on transactions with its associates are eliminated to the extent of the interest in the associates.

## 2.2.3 Adoption of the revaluation model in 'Midstream'

Effective September 30, 2018, the Board of Directors of the Company decided to use within the two models provided for on IAS 16 (Property, plant, and equipment), the 'revaluation model' for the core assets valuation, comprising the gas pipeline systems, including gas pipelines, fields and compressor plants. Additionally,  it  has  been  determined  that  this  group  of  assets  comprises  a  category  of  assets pursuant to IFRS 16, considering the nature, characteristics, and inherent risks.

For the application of such model, associate companies use the services of independent experts which were shared with the Board of Directors which approved the revaluation.  Furthermore, the Board of Directors decides after discussing with experts and associates, the valuation methods and where applicable, the entry data to be used in each case.

Revaluations must be conducted as frequently as necessary so that the value in the accounting records  does  not  differ  significantly  from  the  fair  value  of  the  assets  at  the  date  of  each measurement.

To  measure  the  fair  value  of  assets  subject  to  revaluation,  the  'income  approach'  is  used  as valuation technique as established in IFRS 13 ('Fair Value Measurement ') . The Company uses a model of cash flow discounted prepared on the basis of estimates regarding the future behavior of certain variables which are sensitive in determining the fair value: (i) contracted firm transportation volumes and transportation volumes sold under interruptible transportation mode; (ii) gas carriage tariffs; (iii) operating and maintenance costs; (iv) necessary investment for the maintenance of the  operating  gas  pipeline  maintenance;  (v)  weighted  discount  rate;  and  (vi)  macroeconomic variables such as inflation rate, devaluation rate, etc.

This valuation method is classified under IFRS 13 as hierarchy of fair value Level 3.

## I) Oil and gas exploration activities

The Group applies IFRS 6 'Exploration and evaluation of mineral resources' to account for its oil and gas exploration and evaluation activities.

The amount recorded as provisions is the best estimate of the resource outflow necessary to settle the  present  obligation  at  the  end  of  the  reporting  year,  considering  the  pertinent  risks  and uncertainties.

When a provision is measured using the estimated cash outflow for settling the present obligation, the book value represents the present value of that cash flow.

Provisions recognized by the Group are:

- -Asset retirement obligation and environmental remediation: for the calculation the Company considered the well abandonment plan until the end of the concession and valued them at the estimated  cost  of  abandonment,  discounted  at  the  rate  reflecting  the  specific  risks  of  the liability and the time value of money (Note 2.2.5 II). Asset retirement obligations of the area once operations have been completed led management to make estimates regarding the longterm abandonment costs and the time remaining until abandonment.
- -Provision for gas imbalance: corresponds to the volume of gas due for gas production assigned to the Company in excess of the production resulting from its contractual participation in the Aguaragüe area. ## 2.2.16 Revenue recognition

## 4.3 Fair value estimate

The Group classifies the measurements of financial instruments at fair value using a fair value hierarchy, which reflects the relevance of the variables used to carry out such measurements. The fair value hierarchy has the following levels:

- -Level 1: (Unadjusted) quoted prices in active markets for identical assets or liabilities,
- -Level  2:  Data  other  than  the  quoted  prices  included  in  Level  1  observable  for  assets  or liabilities, whether directly (e.g. prices) or indirectly (e.g. derived from prices),
- -Level 3: Data on assets and liabilities not based on information observable in the market (e.g. not observable information).

The value of financial instruments traded in active markets is based on quoted market prices at the date of the Statement of Financial Position, A market is considered active if quoted prices are regularly  available  through  a  stock  exchange,  financial  intermediary,  industry  institution,  or regulatory body, and those prices reflect current and regular market transactions between parties acting at arm's length. The quoted market price used for financial assets held by the Group is the current bid price. These instruments are included in Level 1.

## Methodology for estimating recoverable values

The methodology used in estimating the recoverable amount consisted of calculating the value in use of each CGU based on the present value of future net cash flow expected to be obtained from the CGU, discounted at a rate reflecting the weighted average cost of capital employed.

The cash flow were prepared based on estimates regarding the future behavior of certain variables that are sensitive in the determination of the value in use, among which are: (i) reference prices for the products; (ii) nature, opportunity and modality of tariff increases; (iii) demand projections by type of product; (iv) evolution of costs; (v) investment needs for the development of the areas; (vi) macroeconomic variables such as inflation rates, exchange rate, among others.

As a result of the updated analysis carried out by the Company as of December 31, 2022, a gain of $1,908,116 was identified, corresponding to a recovery for impairment of non-financial assets of $2,170,028 in the CGUs Dos Hermanos, Cóndor and La Maggie and a loss due to impairment in the  CGU  Maria  Inés  for  ($  261,912).  During  the  year  ended  December  31,  2021,  a  gain  of $5,109,182 was recognized, corresponding to a recovery for impairment of non-financial assets as of December 31, 2021 of $ 5,567,629 in the CGUs Dos Hermanos, Campo Boleadoras, Maria Inés and La Maggie and a loss due to impairment in CGU Cóndor for ($ 458,447). ## Note 6. Segment reporting

IFRS 8 "Segment Reporting" requires an entity to report financial and descriptive information for those operating segments or groupings of segments classified as reportable that meet certain criteria. Operating segments are those where separate financial information is available and are regularly evaluated by the Chief Operating Decision Maker ("CODM") to make choices about how to  allocate  resources  or  analyze  asset  performance.  Operating  segments  are  reported  on  a consistent basis with internal reporting to the CODM or, if applicable, to the Board of Directors.

The Group has two operating and reportable segments, which are organized on the basis of similar economic characteristics, nature of the products offered, production processes, type and classes of customers and distribution methods, as follows:

- -'Upstream': includes results in oil, gas and LPG exploration and production;
- -'Midstream': include results from permanent investments in the following gas transportation companies: Transportadora de Gas del Norte S.A., Transportadora de Gas del Mercosur S.A., and Gasoducto GasAndes S.A. (Argentina), and Gasoducto GasAndes S.A., (Chile).

Core  structure  includes  expenses  common  to  the  reported  segments  such  as  administrative expenses, taxes on financial transactions, interest on financial liabilities and income taxes incurred by the Group in the ordinary course of business, which are not allocated to the reported segments.

The CODM uses Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA with dividends received to make decisions on how to allocate resources and monitor segment performance.

Adjusted  EBITDA  is  defined  as  the  operating  result  of  the  consolidated  segments  excluding depreciation and impairment expenses of property, plant and equipment and right to use lease assets, expected losses on trade and other receivables, provisions for lawsuits and other claims, gains (losses) on return of areas and unsuccessful exploratory investments; taxes on financial transactions.  Adjusted  EBITDA  with  dividends  received  also  includes  dividends  received  on investments in  associates  and  joint  ventures.  Total  adjusted  EBITDA  is  defined  as  the  sum  of adjusted EBITDA of all segments.

## 38.1 Hydrocarbons Law

Hydrocarbon  exploration,  exploitation  and  production  activities  are  regulated  by  the  general framework of Law N° 17,319 (enacted in 1967) and amended by Law N° 26,197, which established that hydrocarbon deposits located in the territory of the Argentine Republic belong to the patrimony of the Provinces, while those located in its continental shelf belong to the Nation.

The general regulatory framework was amended by Law N° 27,007 enacted on October 30, 2014 which, among other provisions established:

-  New  terms  for  non-conventional  hydrocarbon  exploitation  concessions  and  for  exploitation concessions on the continental shelf and in the territorial sea. It also introduced new guidelines for the extension of exploitation concessions.

-  A  new  Investment Promotion Regime for the Exploitation of Hydrocarbons aimed at projects involving direct investment in foreign currency of not less than USD 250 million.

In addition to this general framework, Decree N° 1,277/2012 published on July 25, 2012 regulating Law N° 26,741 (called "Hydrocarbon Sovereignty of the Argentine Republic"), declared the activity of  exploitation  and  transportation  of  hydrocarbons  to be  of national  public  interest.  Decree  N° 272/15 amending Decree N° 1,277/2012, among other changes, dissolved the Commission for Strategic Planning and Coordination of the National Hydrocarbons Investment Plan and repealed certain competences of the Commission and established that the tasks previously assigned to the Commission would be transferred to the -today- National Secretary of Energy.

## 38.2 Crude oil market

In the domestic market, there are currently local prices for the purchase and sale of crude that are unrelated to international market prices. Although there are no regulations that regulate this, the difficulty of moving fuel prices at the pump works as a reference parameter and it is for this reason that local prices are lower than international ones. It is necessary to apply to the Ministry of Energy for  an  export  permit  for  each  spot  sale  or  sale  in  firm  condition  that  must  be  dealt  with.  The regulation that regulates this procedure is Resolution 241/2017.

Currently the DDEE are governed by Art. 7 of the Dto. 488/2020.

## 38.4 Export duties on hydrocarbons

Decree No. 488/2020, established with effect from May 19, 2020 aliquots of export duties. This decree establishes a mathematical formula where the rate varies between 0% and 8% depending on  the  Brent  price.  The  8%  ceiling  coincides  with  the  provisions  of  the  Social  Solidarity  and Productive Reactivation Law No. 27,541 on the matter.

The  determination  of  the  applicable  rate  will  depend  on  the  "ICE  Brent  first  line"  barrel  price (international price or PI).

- -If the PI is equal to or less than 45 dollars (which is set as the base value or VB), the rate will be 0% (ie, no export duties will be paid for the merchandise).
- -If the value of the PI is equal to or greater than 60 dollars (which is set as a reference value or VR), 8% export duties will be paid for the merchandise.
- -If the PI is higher than the VB and lower than the VR, an intermediate value between the VB and the VR will be paid, calculated based on a scale that provides a formula provided in Decree 488/2020 in its Art. 7.

## 38.5 Exchange Regime

By means of DNU N° 609/19 of September 1, 2019, the PEN reestablished the exchange control regime that had been progressively repealed since 2015. During the year 2020, exchange controls became stricter, thus BCRA Communication "A" 7030 (as amended) provided, among other measures:

- -Close access to the MULC for those who, at the time of requesting access have liquid foreign assets for more than USD 100,000 (subject to certain exceptions);
- -Temporarily limiting access to the MULC for the payment of imports of most goods and for the cancellation of principal of debts arising from the importation of goods, establishing a formula that computes the value of goods nationalized during 2020 and the payments of imports and import debts;
- -Temporarily  limiting  access  to  the  MULC  for  the  payment  of  principal  on  foreign  financial indebtedness when the creditor is a counterparty related to the debtor; and
- -Extend to 90 days (i) the term limiting the access to the MULC to those who have made sales transactions of securities with settlement in foreign currency and (ii) the term during which those who have accessed the MULC will not be able to make such transactions with securities.
